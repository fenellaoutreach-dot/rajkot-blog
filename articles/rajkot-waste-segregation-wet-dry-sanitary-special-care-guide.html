Waste Segregation in Rajkot: Wet, Dry, Sanitary & Special-Care Guide

Separating household waste before collection makes it easier to handle food scraps, recyclable materials and items that need extra care. For Rajkot residents, the practical question is no longer just “wet or dry?” India's Solid Waste Management Rules, 2026 specify four streams at source: wet, dry, sanitary and special-care waste.

Quick answer: Keep food scraps separate from clean paper, plastic, metal and glass. Wrap sanitary waste separately. Set aside batteries, bulbs, expired medicines and other special-care items for authorised handling. Ask your local Rajkot Municipal Corporation (RMC) collection team how each stream is accepted in your area; collection arrangements can differ.

Why four streams matter in Rajkot

The Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) describes mandatory four-stream source segregation under the Solid Waste Management Rules, 2026, effective 1 April 2026. RMC is the city's municipal authority responsible for solid-waste services. Historical RMC material and municipal reporting document door-to-door collection and segregation initiatives, but published service statistics are not a guarantee of the exact collection schedule or facility in your ward. Source: CPCB solid-waste portal; RMC official website.

1. Wet waste: food and biodegradable kitchen scraps

Collect fruit and vegetable peels, leftover food, tea leaves and flowers in a separate container. Empty it regularly and keep it covered to limit odour and pests. Avoid mixing packaging, glass or batteries into the wet stream. Households interested in composting should first confirm a suitable method and their housing society's arrangements rather than assuming every apartment has a composting facility.

2. Dry waste: paper, plastic, metal and glass

Set aside paper, cardboard, plastic packaging, metal containers and glass where accepted by the local collection system. Empty containers and keep paper as dry as practical. Broken glass needs careful packaging and clear communication with the authorised collector so nobody handling it is injured. Not every plastic item is recyclable locally: the dry category is not a promise that every material will be recycled.

3. Sanitary waste: wrap and separate

Used diapers, sanitary napkins and similar hygiene products belong in a separate sanitary-waste stream under the national framework. Wrap them securely according to the product instructions and local collection guidance. Do not put them with food scraps or loose recyclable paper. Ask the RMC collection service about the accepted handover method.

4. Special-care waste: do not mix with ordinary rubbish

Items such as used batteries, discarded bulbs, expired medicines and certain chemical containers require separate attention. Keep them away from children and food-storage areas, do not break or open them, and use authorised collection or take-back arrangements. Medical sharps and clinical waste can have additional rules; contact the appropriate authorised service rather than putting them into a household recycling bag. The CPCB's four-stream explanation lists representative special-care items: official classification.

A simple household setup

  1. Choose four clearly labelled containers or secure collection areas. They need not match the colours in our illustration.
  2. Place the wet-waste container near food preparation and the dry-waste container near packaging disposal.
  3. Keep sanitary and special-care materials separate and safely secured until handover.
  4. Explain the system to everyone in the household, including helpers and visitors.
  5. Confirm the collection vehicle's actual segregation procedure with the local authorised team before putting materials out.

What shops, cafés and housing societies should check

Restaurants, cafés, retail shops and residential societies may generate a different mix and volume of waste than a single home. The CPCB's 2026 portal also identifies additional responsibilities for qualifying bulk waste generators. Do not assume a business qualifies based on its category alone: the portal sets out thresholds and eligibility criteria. Commercial operators should check current RMC instructions and whether separate arrangements are required for food waste, packaging and other waste streams. CPCB bulk-waste generator information.

What to do if collection is missed or instructions are unclear

Start with your building's designated collection contact or ward office, noting the location and date. For official municipal contact details and civic service information, use Rajkot Municipal Corporation's website. Do not rely on an unverified phone number circulated in a social-media post. Collection timing, pickup method and special drives may change.

Frequently asked questions

Are wet and dry bins enough in 2026?

The national 2026 rules describe four streams: wet, dry, sanitary and special-care. Follow RMC's current instructions for how those streams are collected locally.

Can I put batteries in the dry-waste bag?

No. Used batteries are a special-care item and should be kept separately for authorised handling.

Do the four categories require four particular bin colours?

This article does not assert a Rajkot-specific colour code. Labels and correct separation matter; confirm any prescribed container colours with RMC or your authorised collector.

Does Rajkot offer door-to-door waste collection?

Municipal and government reporting describe RMC's door-to-door collection operations. Exact service coverage, collection days and arrangements should be verified for your street or society rather than assumed from citywide reporting.
