Famous Food in Rajkot: 10 Local Dishes, Snacks & Sweets

Updated 28 November 2026 · Rajkot.blog editorial team

Rajkot is famous for Kathiyawadi thali, bajra rotla, sev tameta, ringan no olo, gathiya and fafda-jalebi. For sweets, look for penda. These favourites reflect the food culture of Saurashtra and wider Gujarat; the guide below explains what to order and how to explore local food.

Not every item on this list originated in Rajkot: several are favourites throughout Gujarat. Together, they offer a useful introduction to the region's everyday food culture.

What food is Rajkot famous for?

For a first taste of Rajkot, start with a Kathiyawadi thali, bajra rotla with ringan no olo, sev tameta, gathiya and fafda-jalebi. For something sweet, try penda. These are regional favourites rather than dishes invented exclusively in Rajkot. The city's place in Saurashtra helps explain the prominence of millet breads, garlic, vegetables and bold seasoning.

1. Kathiyawadi Thali

A Kathiyawadi thali brings together several dishes in one meal. Depending on the kitchen and season, it may include rotla, vegetable preparations, dal or kadhi, rice, pickles, chutneys and buttermilk. It is a practical introduction to the bold, varied flavours associated with the Saurashtra region.

2. Bajra Rotla

Bajra rotla is a rustic flatbread made with pearl millet flour. It is often served warm with ghee, jaggery, garlic chutney or a vegetable dish. The earthy flavour and hearty texture make it a familiar part of traditional Gujarati and Kathiyawadi meals.

3. Sev Tameta nu Shaak

This comforting preparation combines tomatoes with spices and crunchy sev. The sev softens as it meets the gravy, giving the dish a distinctive texture. It is commonly enjoyed with rotli, rotla or other flatbreads.

4. Ringan no Olo

Ringan no olo is a smoky mashed eggplant dish, typically prepared using roasted brinjal, onions, tomatoes and spices. It pairs especially well with bajra rotla and is a classic flavour to look for in Kathiyawadi-style meals.

5. Lasaniya Bataka

As the name suggests, garlic is central to lasaniya bataka, a spiced potato dish with a punchy, savoury character. Versions vary in heat and richness, so ask about spice levels if you prefer milder food.

6. Dhokla

Soft, steamed dhokla is a popular Gujarati snack often served with chutney. While it is enjoyed across Gujarat rather than being exclusive to Rajkot, it is an easy breakfast or teatime option to try during a visit.

7. Khandvi

Khandvi is made from thin, delicate rolls of a seasoned gram-flour and yogurt mixture. A tempering of mustard seeds and other aromatics adds flavour. Its light texture makes it a good contrast to richer Kathiyawadi dishes.

8. Fafda and Jalebi

Crisp, savoury fafda paired with sweet jalebi is a much-loved Gujarati combination, particularly associated with festive mornings. It offers a memorable mix of crunchy, sweet and salty flavours.

9. Gathiya

Gathiya is a crunchy or tender gram-flour snack, depending on the style. It is widely enjoyed with tea and can be found in many Gujarati snack shops. Freshness makes a noticeable difference, so consider a busy shop with frequent batches.

10. Chaas (Buttermilk)

Chaas is a refreshing yogurt-based drink, sometimes seasoned with salt, roasted cumin or herbs. It commonly accompanies Gujarati meals and provides a cooling balance to spicier dishes.

Tips for Exploring Food in Rajkot

  • Try a traditional Kathiyawadi thali to sample several dishes at once.
  • Ask about chilli levels: recipes and spice preferences vary between kitchens.
  • Look for freshly prepared snacks, and check ingredients if you have allergies or dietary requirements.
  • Menus, prices and opening hours can change; confirm details directly with restaurants or shops before visiting.

Frequently Asked Questions

What food is Rajkot known for?

Rajkot is closely associated with Kathiyawadi food culture. Rotla, ringan no olo, sev tameta and traditional thalis are good starting points, alongside Gujarati snacks such as gathiya.

What are the famous snacks and sweets of Rajkot?

Start with gathiya, fafda-jalebi, dhokla and khandvi, then try penda if you want something sweet to take home. Our farsan buying guide and penda guide explain freshness, packing and what to ask at a shop.

What should I order on my first visit?

Choose a Kathiyawadi thali for a full meal, or gathiya and jalebi for a snack. If you have only a day, follow the one-day food trail for a flexible itinerary.

Is Kathiyawadi food always very spicy?

Many Kathiyawadi dishes have bold seasoning, but heat varies by recipe and restaurant. You can ask for a milder preparation where available.

Can vegetarians find plenty of options?

Yes. Many traditional Gujarati and Kathiyawadi dishes are vegetarian, although it is still worth checking ingredients and preparation practices for individual dietary needs.
