Rajkot Civic Body Seeks ₹18.73 Lakh in Jangleshwar Demolition Bill Recoveries

RMC plans to recover or withhold nearly ₹19 lakh following a review of February's Jangleshwar demolition expenses. What the November reports establish and what remains unresolved.

Rajkot Municipal Corporation (RMC) is seeking to protect approximately ₹18.73 lakh in public funds following an internal review of expenditure linked to February's clearance operation in Jangleshwar. The latest development concerns bills submitted for services during the demolition drive along the Aji River, rather than a new demolition operation.

According to The Indian Express's report of 26 November, the amount identified for recovery or deduction is ₹18,72,561. The newspaper describes a review of expenses involving three municipal departments and seven service providers. The Times of India's report on the same date also describes the civic body's acknowledgment of excess expenditure and the initiation of disciplinary proceedings.

What is being reviewed?

The February operation required support services beyond the physical removal of structures. The recent reporting identifies expenses associated with temporary shelters, refreshments, vehicles and recording work. The subsequent scrutiny concerns whether amounts claimed or paid for those services were justified.

It is important to distinguish bills still awaiting settlement from money that has already left municipal accounts. A deduction can prevent a disputed amount from being paid, whereas recovering an earlier payment requires an additional step. The reported total should not be interpreted as proof that the entire sum has already been returned to the civic body.

Administrative action after the inquiry

Times of India and Gujarat Samachar report that the municipal administration has suspended one official and initiated departmental inquiries concerning other officials. These are administrative proceedings, not final findings of personal criminal liability. The outcome of those proceedings and the final amount recovered remain to be established.

The July food bill and the November recovery figure refer to different reported stages of review. Without the underlying accounting records, adding these figures together would risk double counting.

Why this matters to Rajkot residents

Public procurement accountability extends beyond large construction contracts. Temporary services arranged for civic operations also need documented orders, evidence of delivery and appropriate payment approval. When a bill is challenged, residents benefit from a clear distinction between the amount questioned, the amount disallowed and the amount actually recovered.

RMC's public budget and audit information page is a useful starting point for readers interested in municipal finances. It is a general official resource, not the investigation report for this case.

What remains unanswered

Rajkot.blog has not obtained the full internal inquiry report, individual payment records or final departmental orders. We have not independently confirmed that all recoveries are complete. Further official disclosures would help clarify the actual sums returned, the final decisions on disputed invoices and the outcome of the disciplinary process.
