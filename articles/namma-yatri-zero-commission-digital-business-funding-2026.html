Namma Yatri Raises ₹27.25 Crore: Inside India's Zero-Commission Ride-Hailing Business

Rajkot.blog editorial team · 28 November 2026 · India Digital Business

What happens when a ride-booking app stops taking a percentage of the fare? Bengaluru's Namma Yatri is testing a different answer to the economics of digital marketplaces. On 27 November 2026, Entrackr reported a ₹27.25 crore investment in its operator, Moving Tech, as part of an ongoing pre-Series A round. A separate financial report published a day earlier put FY26 operating revenue at ₹37.5 crore. Together, the developments make this an interesting case of digital growth, even though the business is not yet profitable.

What happened on 27 November?

The financing report identifies the Michael & Susan Dell Foundation as contributing ₹27 crore and Narayana Hrudayalaya Limited as contributing ₹25 lakh. Venture Intelligence's November 27 coverage also reports the transaction. These are reported investment figures, not money earned from passengers. We have not independently obtained the underlying corporate filings.

How a ride-hailing app can operate without a percentage commission

Namma Yatri started with auto-rickshaw bookings in 2022. Its approach separates the fare paid by a passenger from the service charge for using the software. The company website describes a platform connecting drivers and passengers directly. In principle, that means the driver's fare is not reduced by a percentage commission from the platform. But operating an app still costs money.

The company's driver plan documentation, updated in June 2026, explains two fee structures: a fixed daily charge for unlimited bookings or a daily charge based on completed ride activity. Details can differ by location and vehicle category. Subscription income helps support the technology needed for mapping, infrastructure and operations. Zero commission does not mean zero cost for the driver or that every passenger ride is cheaper.

Funding may finance expansion; it does not erase operating losses or prove that future growth will generate a profit.

Why the model is interesting for digital entrepreneurs

Digital marketplaces face a basic challenge: both buyers and sellers must find enough value in the service to return. A subscription can make charges easier to anticipate for frequent users, while usage-based billing may suit someone who works less often. Neither is automatically the better choice. The platform must demonstrate that its software helps users earn or save enough to justify what they pay.

Consider a hypothetical marketplace that connects local service providers with customers. Rather than taking a share of every job, its operator could test a flat fee for useful business tools. The important question would be whether sellers renew voluntarily after seeing measurable benefits. Building an app before testing the value of the service could lead to substantial costs without enough paying customers.

Open software is part of the story

The project maintains a public open-source repository on GitHub. For a software founder, public code can make technical collaboration possible. It does not remove the expenses of support, hosting, mapping, payment reliability or customer acquisition. A developer exploring this approach should review the actual software license, infrastructure needs and local rules rather than assume that copying a repository creates a viable company.

What Rajkot businesses can learn

Local founders could test simple booking, appointment or enquiry tools for businesses before investing in a citywide marketplace. Interview potential paying users, define exactly what the fee covers, and compare acquisition costs with renewals. Our digital marketing guide and small business planning guide offer starting points. This is analysis, not a claim that Namma Yatri currently operates in Rajkot.

FAQs

Is Namma Yatri profitable?

No, the latest FY26 figures cited above show a net loss of ₹43.8 crore.

How does it make money without percentage commissions?

It charges drivers for platform access through applicable subscription or usage-based plans, as described in its help centre.

Is the ₹27.25 crore its annual income?

No. It is a reported funding transaction dated 27 November 2026, separate from operating revenue.
